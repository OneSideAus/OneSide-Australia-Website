Worth Knowing

Taking child safety to a whole association, not just one club

Sporting club committees sit alongside associations and leagues, and everyone carries a different layer of responsibility when it comes to child safety. OneSide Australia is now working with Melbourne Eastern Netball Association (MENA) on a new way to bring those layers together: a Child Safety Forum, hosted by OneSide and co-hosted by MENA's own Child Safety Officer, bringing every affiliated club's Child Safety Officer and President into one room.

It's built to do two things at once. For the clubs, it's a chance to speak directly with an industry expert in child safety compliance, and to compare notes with other Child Safety Officers across the association — what's working, where they're stuck, and how they can help each other, rather than each club working out its gaps alone. For MENA, it becomes a standing part of the association's annual child safety review: a direct, in-person read on where every club actually stands, not just what's reported on paper.

This is the foundation of how OneSide works with leagues and associations, not a one-off exclusive to MENA. Any league or association ready for the same clarity across every one of its clubs can bring the Forum to theirs by emailing info@onesideaustralia.com.au.

What's Changed
NSW · Cricket

Cricket NSW's Fair Play Declaration is now in effect

Every Cricket NSW-affiliated club is now expected to formally endorse and sign the CNSW Fair Play Declaration (dated no earlier than 1 July 2026), publish it along with the Australian Cricket Code of Behaviour and Safeguarding Policy on their club website or socials, and appoint a Child Safety Officer. It's a timely reminder that the same fundamentals — a named CSO, published policies, a way to raise concerns — apply everywhere, not just in Victoria. It's also why cricket is where we're headed next: OneSide is about to start working with cricket clubs for the first time, and we'll have more to share on that soon.

From the Field

Not just players

On the ground as water carrier for ENFL — a good reminder that child safety protections don't stop at the players. Runners, water carriers, umpires, coaches and assistant coaches are often filled by under-18s too, and the same duty of care applies to them as it does to anyone on your team list.

It's a bigger group than most clubs realise. A record 20,000+ community umpires took the field in AFL alone in 2024, up 18% in a single year — and that growth is being driven partly by teenagers stepping up to fill the gap.

Worth checking at your own club: does your register of children in child-facing roles include your junior umpires, runners and water carriers — not just your junior players? And if a 15-year-old is running the boundary or timekeeping on Saturday, do they know who to go to if something doesn't feel right, the same as any player would?
