What's in your register

Every gap becomes a tracked, ownable risk.

Here's exactly what your committee receives.

Every requirement, one line at a time

Each minimum requirement of your state's child safe standards gets its own row, with a Result already filled in from your gap report: Met, Partially Met, Not Met or Opportunity for Improvement.

Uses the same Result wording as the regulator's own self-assessment tool, so your register can stand in for it.

Risks rated by likelihood and consequence

For each requirement, the register describes what could realistically go wrong for a child at your club, then rates it on likelihood and consequence. The rating is colour coded so the urgent items stand out.

Highest priorityHighMediumLow

What you already do, and what to do next

Your existing controls are recorded alongside recommended actions drawn from your gap report, so your committee can see what's already in place and the next practical step for each risk.

Options, not mandates. Your committee decides what to take on and when.

Owners, dates and status, ready to track

Assign an owner and a target date to each risk, then update the Status as you go: Not Started, In Progress or Complete. A tracking summary counts your progress automatically.

Includes a "How to use this register" tab for your committee.

How you get it

Three steps to your Risk Register.

No extra forms. Your register is built from the assessment you're already doing.

1

Choose the bundle

Select Assessment + Risk Register at checkout, then complete your assessment as normal. It takes around 30 to 40 minutes.

2

Get your gap report

Your gap report is generated as soon as you finish, with findings against every standard and practical options for each gap.

3

Your register follows

Straight after your report, your Risk Register is built from its findings. You can download it on screen, and it's also emailed to you as an Excel spreadsheet.

Built for committee meetings

Turn child safety into a standing agenda item.

A gap report is a snapshot. A risk register is how you keep moving. Bring it to each committee or child safety meeting, work through the highest rated risks first, and record what was decided.

When your annual reassessment comes around, you'll have a clear record of what your club has done.

Confirm an owner for each risk, by role or by name
Work through Highest priority and High risks first
Set target dates so owners know when to report back
Update the status as actions are completed
Use the notes column to record decisions and dates
Review the whole register at least once a season

Your committee makes the decisions.
